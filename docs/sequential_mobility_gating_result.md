# Sequential mobility gating: internal readiness gates initiation, completion is context dependent

## Biological question

Does realised migration emerge through two separable gates?

1. **internal initiation gate** — is the eel physiologically/morphologically ready to enter the migratory movement regime?
2. **external completion gate** — once migration begins, can the individual progress successfully through the landscape?

This decomposition is more specific than a generic stage × landscape interaction.

## Gate 1 — migration initiation

Across six public European eel project-level migration tables:

- FIII: 161/261 initiated = **61.7%**
- FIV: 54/68 initiated = **79.4%**
- FV: 216/246 initiated = **87.8%**

The project × release-year fixed-effects model additionally controls within-stratum body length and release timing.

Durif effect per one-stage increment:

- OR = **1.99**
- 95% CI = **1.49–2.66**
- p = **3.2e-6**

Thus advanced capture-time migratory readiness strongly predicts whether later behavioural migration is expressed.

## Gate 2 — successful completion among initiators

Conditional on having entered the migration state:

- FIII: 106/161 successful = **65.8%**
- FIV: 45/54 successful = **83.3%**
- FV: 116/216 successful = **53.7%**

The raw pattern is clearly not monotonic with stage.

Project × release-year fixed-effects model among initiators:

- Durif OR per stage increment = **1.29**
- 95% CI = **0.94–1.77**
- p = **0.121**

Body length is also unsupported:

- OR per 100 mm = **1.01**
- 95% CI = **0.71–1.43**

Therefore the strong stage gradient seen at initiation is much weaker after migration has already begun.

## Albertkanaal diagnostic

The clearest internal example is the high-resistance Genk release context (WRS impact score 14).

### Initiation

- FIII: **25/25**
- FV: **97/101**

Almost all individuals enter the migratory state.

### Successful completion

- FIII: **6/25 initiators = 24%**
- FV: **15/97 initiators = 15.5%**

Thus advanced readiness cannot by itself rescue completion in this context.

This does **not** prove that WRS score 14 caused the attrition. Tracking geometry, route structure and other project-level differences remain possible explanations.

But it directly shows that:

> **movement initiation and movement completion are biologically distinct outcomes.**

## Cross-project completion heterogeneity

Completion among initiators differs sharply among projects.

Examples:

- Albertkanaal: roughly **18%** pooled completion among stage-coded initiators;
- 2015 Scheldt system: roughly **60–91%** depending on stage;
- Grote Nete: **100%** among observed stage-coded initiators.

The relevant next question is therefore not whether advanced stage always produces higher final success.

It is:

> **which external contexts convert internally initiated migration into successful progression, and which create post-initiation bottlenecks?**

## Current ecological proposition

The strongest result now is:

> **Realised migration is sequentially gated: internal migratory readiness strongly controls entry into the migratory movement regime, while successful completion is much more context dependent and is not explained by a simple monotonic readiness gradient.**

This is a movement-ecology result about the separation of motivation/readiness from realised landscape passage.

## What remains to identify

The external completion gate is not yet mechanistically identified.

Candidate external components include:

- barrier structure;
- discharge / hydrological passage opportunity;
- tidal access;
- route geometry;
- tracking-array closure;
- human-controlled water infrastructure.

The Dutch consecutive pump -> tidal-sluice system remains the priority confirmation because the same individuals experience two different external passage contexts.

## Evidence boundary

- The Europe-wide outcome data were inspected during hypothesis refinement.
- This is developmental independent evidence.
- Project/system effects cannot yet be equated with landscape resistance.
- The claim is **sequential gating**, not proof of a particular barrier mechanism.
