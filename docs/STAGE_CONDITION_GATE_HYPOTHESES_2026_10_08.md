# Where does capture body condition enter the silvering-to-migration decision?

**Status: exploratory hypotheses; stage-specific held-out audit is queued.**
Do not cite these hypotheses as demonstrated mechanisms.

## Already observed independently of this stage follow-up

Six-project, 575-eel development panel:
- same-project-year initiation ranking improves from AUC 0.610 to 0.643 when weight-for-length residual is added to Durif, body length and release timing;
- the paired benefit is positive in 4/6 held-out projects and negative in 2/6;
- fish-level fixed-training conditional CI for AUC gain is 0.0021–0.0644;
- six-project resampling interval is **−0.0007–0.0918**, so cross-river generalization is not established;
- post-activation transit-speed indicators do not show a convincing positive transfer of that same entry state.

The issue is **not** whether silvering predicts readiness at all; it does. The unresolved ecological question is what additional body-state variation describes *within* a silvering class and how environmental context changes its importance.

## Competing explanations and discriminating predictions

### H1. Residual readiness gate in less-advanced silvering classes

Within FIII, visually similar silver eels span greater readiness to initiate. Body condition might describe physiological development or energetic potential not completely captured by morphology.

- Observable prediction: within-FIII initiation ranking from condition exceeds within-FV ranking, and that difference retains its sign across projects with informative FIII and FV outcomes.
- Contradiction: benefits are equally strong at FV or vary in direction among projects.
- Limitation: condition is an adjusted weight-for-length residual, **not measured energy reserves**. FIII differences may represent age, sex, maturation or capture phenology, not energy gating.

### H2. Conditional demand at terminal silvering

Morphological advancement may be necessary but not sufficient. Condition may instead discriminate within FV when terminal-stage individuals differ in energy budget or behavioral competence.

- Observable prediction: condition improves initiation concordance within FV more than within FIII in held-out projects.
- Contradiction: the reverse stage pattern is stable across projects.
- Limitation: sparse non-initiation at FV may make a large estimated stage effect imprecise.

### H3. Context-dependent activation (external cue gating)

The same phenotype could predict initiation differently in waterways with different discharge, tide, barriers or receiver layouts. A project-specific response could be due to context rather than stage.

- Observable prediction: stage-specific AUC increments show sign reversals across projects or their between-project intervals cross zero.
- Distinguishing tests (not currently feasible with the freeze): time-varying discharge / sluice events aligned to individual first-onset risk periods, and joint state×cue prediction using withheld fish or independent waterways.
- Important: merely observing heterogeneity cannot identify **which** environmental driver causes it.

### H4. Body size, classification or observation artifact

A weight-for-length residual adjusted for stage and project could still co-vary with sex, age, tagging geometry or migration-detection opportunity.

- Observable prediction: apparent condition gain changes after controlling deployment / detection efficiency, sex where observed, and condition calibration from strictly training-only morphometrics.
- Limitation: these controls are not all available in the public six-project panel; only the impact on ranking can currently be audited, not the mechanism.

## Fixed inference rules for analysis/37

1. Report each FIII/FIV/FV initiation and non-initiation count, informative project count, independent within-project-year pair count, and stage-specific AUC for baseline/expanded models.
2. Report the condition-only within-stage AUC as a descriptive measure, but do not infer independent energy physiology.
3. Show project-level bootstrap intervals and stage contrasts; interpret a small or unsupported stratum as **unresolved**, not null.
4. Do not name any named river as being hydrologically constrained, high-quality or a reservoir of energetic advantage without independent source evidence.
5. Only use a stage-specific sign and CI as motivation for a mechanistic hypothesis; **causal proof requires longitudinal physiology and environmental time series**, or a direct manipulation.

## Conservation meaning (conditional, not yet a result)

A real phenotype-dependent initiation gate would mean the number of potential migrants is not directly interchangeable with the number starting downstream migration. Monitoring should distinguish (i) production and condition of the silver-eel cohort, (ii) probability and timing of initiation, (iii) safe passage/survival after initiation.

The present data demonstrate stronger information for (ii) than for generic speed, **not** quantified escapement, population fitness, barrier mortality or a tested conservation intervention. Avoid treating speed as a substitute for passage success.
