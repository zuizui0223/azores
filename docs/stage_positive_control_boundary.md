# Durif stage as a positive control, not the novelty claim

## Why this correction is necessary

Durif's original silvering classification is biologically ordered by migratory status:

- FII: resident female;
- FIII: pre-migrant female;
- FIV and FV: migrating female stages.

Therefore a result showing:

> FIV/FV eels are more likely than FIII eels to initiate or complete downstream migration

is biologically expected.

The strong Europe-wide associations in this repository are useful, but they are **not the paper's novelty claim**.

## Role of the Europe-wide stage result

The project-fixed and project-year robustness analyses serve as a **positive control**:

1. capture-time morphology contains biologically coherent readiness information;
2. the readiness variable is not erased by body-size or broad release-timing control;
3. the strength of its relationship with realised movement varies strongly among projects.

The third point motivates the actual ecological problem.

## Actual novelty target

> **Why does equivalent or advanced migratory readiness translate into successful movement in some landscape contexts but not others?**

This is a state-dependent constraint question.

Conceptually:

~~~text
realised movement
  = internal readiness
    × hydrological opportunity
    × barrier permeability
    × route/context
~~~

The paper does not need to rediscover that FIV/FV are migratory stages.

It needs to test whether **external opportunity controls the conversion of readiness into realised movement**.

## Current evidence for context dependence

Project-specific advanced-stage versus FIII odds ratios are strongly heterogeneous, including:

- clear advanced-stage advantages in several low/moderate-resistance projects;
- little or reversed advantage in some others.

This heterogeneity is real in the data but cannot be assigned causally to WRS from the Europe-wide panel because resistance is strongly project-confounded.

Thus:

- internal-state effect = positive control;
- between-project heterogeneity = hypothesis-generating;
- state × landscape mechanism = unresolved;
- within-system confirmation = required.

## Required independent design

The ideal next system contains:

- multiple FIII/FIV/FV individuals;
- the same waterway / barrier architecture for all individuals;
- time-varying passage opportunities measured independently;
- movement initiation, delay, passage or failure;
- body size/condition;
- enough stage variation within the same external context.

The Dutch pump -> tidal-sluice dataset is currently the best open-data candidate.

## Claim rule

Do not headline the project as:

> advanced silvering stage predicts migration.

That relationship is expected from the definition and established biology of the Durif classification.

Headline only a supported **interaction or constraint on movement expression** that goes beyond the staging definition.
