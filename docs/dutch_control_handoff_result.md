# Independent Dutch result: control shifts after migration begins

## Current interpretation boundary (2026-10-07)

This file records an earlier **control-handoff hypothesis** and is retained for provenance. It is not the current manuscript-level claim.

The canonical interpretation is the narrower **transferability boundary**:

> **Capture-time silvering readiness is a strong and transferable predictor of migration activation/onset in the six-project cohort, but it provides little general predictive information for pooled whole-route post-activation speed.**

The pooled speed endpoint reproduces exactly (n=418; ratio 0.983, 95% CI 0.852–1.134), and project-specific speed coefficients show no detectable heterogeneity (Q=2.47, df=5, p=0.781). This does **not** establish that internal state becomes irrelevant after activation or that external opportunity universally takes over control. A River Test study published in 2026 provides an explicit external boundary where silvering stage contributes to a route-specific progression metric.

## External system

van Rijn, Kuipers & Huisman (2026) followed 40 European eels through a pumping station and a later tidal sluice in the same Dutch source-to-sea route.

Tagged animals were already in Durif FIII-FV:

- FIII: 11
- FIV: 6
- FV: 23

Thus this system is not a yellow-versus-silver initiation experiment. It is an **already activated / near-activated migration** system.

Published progression:

- 37/40 reached the pumping station;
- 35 passed it;
- 36 reached the lake;
- 27 passed the tidal sluice and reached the sea;
- cumulative barrier delay among successful migrants averaged about 34 days.

## What happened to Durif stage?

### Pumping station

The individual-level global model initially included:

- weight;
- condition factor;
- log canal migration speed;
- Durif stage.

Durif stage was **dropped during model selection**.

The published final individual-level signal instead emphasized:

- faster canal migration -> fewer missed passage opportunities;
- better body condition -> more missed passage opportunities.

At the event level, passage increased with:

- longer discharge duration;
- higher wind speed.

### Tidal sluice

Durif stage was **excluded from analysis** because successful FIV individuals were substantially heavier than other stages, making maturity and body mass non-identifiable.

At the event level, lower moon illumination increased passage.

Prior experience also mattered:

- eels with more passage opportunities at the pumping station subsequently accumulated fewer missed passage opportunities at the tidal sluice.

## Ecological interpretation

This independent system does **not** support a simple universal rule:

> more advanced Durif stage -> easier passage at every barrier.

Instead it is compatible with a phase-dependent handoff:

~~~text
before / at migration activation
    internal readiness matters strongly
             |
             v
after active migration begins
    local hydrology, barrier operation,
    movement state and prior passage experience
    increasingly govern progression
~~~

This is consistent with the Europe-wide result that Durif stage strongly predicts **whether/when migration begins**, while its general advantage weakens after initiation.

## Stronger hypothesis

> **The dominant controller of realised movement changes across movement phases: internal state gates activation, whereas external opportunity and route history govern progression after activation.**

Call this provisionally **initiation–progression control handoff**.

The name is descriptive only; novelty must come from empirical phase separation, not terminology.

## What remains unresolved

The Dutch paper cannot fully test a Durif × opportunity interaction because:

- stage was dropped at the pumping-station individual model;
- stage was confounded with body mass at the tidal sluice;
- n is only 40.

Therefore the external result is a **constraint and partial corroboration**, not definitive confirmation.

## Implication for further data work

Raw DANS data remain useful, but the purpose changes.

Do not use them to rescue a stage effect.

If obtained, use them to estimate:

1. how much variance in passage is explained by individual readiness after active migration is underway;
2. how much is explained by event-level opportunity;
3. whether prior barrier experience predicts the next barrier after individual traits are controlled.

A null Durif effect is scientifically informative under this revised hypothesis.
