# Figure captions — Azores phase-control manuscript

## Figure 1. Internal migratory readiness is evaluated at sequential movement phases

**(A)** Capture-time Durif silvering stage provides the ordinal internal-readiness axis used in the primary analysis (FIII → FIV → FV).  
**(B)** Conceptual decomposition of seaward migration into migration activation followed by post-activation progression and completion. The same independently measured internal-state predictor is evaluated at both gates.  
**(C)** Six-project analysis universe contributing stage-resolved migration records. The schematic defines the inferential structure and is not itself an effect-size result.

## Figure 2. Advanced Durif stage predicts entry into migration

**(A)** Expert-corrected stage-specific migration-initiation fractions across the primary six-project cohort: FIII, 154/261 (59.0%); FIV, 53/68 (77.9%); FV, 215/246 (87.4%). Fractions are descriptive; adjusted inference accounts for project × release-year context, body length and release timing.  
**(B)** Hazard ratio for migration onset per one-stage FIII → FIV → FV increment from the stratified Cox analysis. The primary hazard ratio was **1.28** (95% CI **1.12–1.45**, *p* = **0.00022**). Leave-one-project-out estimates are shown as robustness diagnostics. Positive direction was retained across all six deletions, although precision weakened when the 2015 project was excluded.

## Figure 3. The predictive effect of internal readiness attenuates after migration has begun

**(A)** Adjusted ordinal Durif-stage effects at the two sequential gates. At migration initiation, the odds ratio per stage increment was **2.08**; conditional on initiation, the completion odds ratio was **1.15**.  
**(B)** Direct stacked-model contrast of the two stage coefficients. The initiation-to-completion odds-ratio ratio was **1.81** (cluster-robust 95% CI **1.15–2.84**, *p* = **0.0099**). Leave-one-project-out ratios retained the same direction in all six deletions. The result supports a phase-dependent change in relative predictive control; it does not imply that internal state becomes biologically irrelevant after initiation.  
**(C)** Post-initiation migration speed by Durif stage. The adjusted multiplicative speed ratio per stage increment was **0.983** (95% CI **0.852–1.134**, *p* = **0.815**). Speed is a secondary progression endpoint and is not used to redefine internal migratory state.

## Figure 4. Project-level landscape resistance aligns with completion, not initiation

Six project contexts are shown against median published water-regulating-structure (WRS) impact.  
**(A)** WRS impact versus migration-initiation rate showed essentially no rank association (Spearman ρ = **0.029**, exact *p* = **0.983**).  
**(B)** WRS impact versus completion conditional on initiation showed a strong negative rank association (Spearman ρ = **−0.928**, exact *p* = **0.022**). Points are labelled project contexts rather than experimental treatments. Because WRS exposure is strongly project-confounded and *n* = 6, these panels are bridge/descriptive evidence only and do not establish a causal effect of WRS.

## Figure 5. Independent consecutive-barrier evidence constrains post-activation movement

Schematic of the independent Dutch source-to-sea route reported by van Rijn et al. (2026): pumping station → lake → tidal sluice → sea. Of **40** tagged European eels, **35** passed the pumping station and **27** completed seaward passage through the tidal sluice; mean cumulative barrier delay was approximately **34 d**. Published barrier-specific effects of discharge opportunity, wind, lunar conditions and prior passage experience are annotated at the relevant route stages. This system provides independent ecological corroboration that post-activation progress is filtered by route-specific opportunity; it is not a direct replication of the Europe-wide phase-interaction estimate.
