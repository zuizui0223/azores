# Figure captions V4 — multivariate entry-state manuscript

## Figure 1. Capture state is tested across successive phases of seaward migration

**(A)** Capture phenotype is represented by ordinal female Durif stage
(FIII→FIV→FV) and a continuous weight-for-length condition proxy. **(B)** The
same pre-movement information is evaluated against migration activation,
threshold-defined behavioral onset, whole-route speed after activation and a
frozen per-eel median positive inter-station speed endpoint. **(C)** Sample
sizes differ by estimand: 575 evaluable stage-coded tracks, 525 animals in
informative multivariate activation strata, 570 animals with 418 onset events,
418 whole-route speed records and 411 frozen segment-speed records. Terminal
receiver non-detection is not represented as biological failure.

## Figure 2. Migration entry contains capture-state information beyond ordinal silvering stage

**(A)** Expert-corrected activation fractions were 154/261 (59.0%) for FIII,
53/68 (77.9%) for FIV and 215/246 (87.4%) for FV. **(B)** In the multivariate
activation model, the odds ratio was **2.130** per Durif-stage increment
(95% CI **1.596–2.842**) and **1.460** per SD of capture weight-for-length
condition (95% CI **1.164–1.832**). **(C)** The activation-trained entry-state
score used fixed weights 0.756 for ordinal Durif stage and 0.379 for capture
condition; the two terms jointly improved activation fit by LR χ²(2)=**39.01**.
Condition is a biometric proxy and is not interpreted as an independent
physiological reserve measurement.

## Figure 3. An activation-trained entry-state score transfers to onset but not realized post-activation transit speed

Per 1 SD of the score trained only on migration activation, activation odds
increased **2.230-fold** (95% CI **1.704–2.918**) and behavioral-onset hazard
increased **1.317-fold** (95% CI **1.168–1.485**). The same score showed little
association with whole-route speed after activation (ratio **0.946**,
95% CI **0.832–1.076**; partial R² **0.18%**) or the independently frozen
median positive inter-station speed endpoint (ratio **1.028**,
95% CI **0.869–1.215**; partial R² **0.026%**). Odds ratios, hazard ratios and
speed ratios are shown together only to visualize transferability relative to
their common null value of one; their numerical magnitudes are not treated as
directly comparable effect-size scales.

**(B)** A frozen exact-link audit compared positive source-derived movement
speeds within identical directed receiver-to-receiver links, using
project-held-out score weights, fish-equal weighting and fish-clustered
uncertainty. Across **17,792** segments from **418** eels and **244** directed
pairs, the entry-state speed ratio was **0.962/SD** (95% CI **0.883–1.048**;
p=**0.372**; weighted partial R² **0.0382%**). Because the raw source speed
field included biologically impossible values, explicitly post-hoc
external-plausibility screens at 2.5, 5 and 10 m s⁻¹ were applied as
data-quality sensitivities; the corresponding ratios were **0.942**, **0.944**
and **0.941**, with all intervals spanning one. These screens removed
**53.0–63.1%** of candidate raw segment rows and therefore document a major
source-metric quality boundary rather than replacing the frozen primary.


All within-link numbers use the corrected expert-eligible cohort; an earlier implementation inadvertently retained nine expert-classified non-migrants. The post-hoc speed-screen estimates have p≈0.07–0.08, remain compatible with no association and do not establish a negative effect. SVD/Frisch–Waugh–Lovell residualization reproduced the corrected coefficient.

## Figure 4. Arrival-defined Dutch barrier data do not support a general condition-dependent passage-selectivity mechanism

**(A)** Primary passage-event risk sets were defined from observed barrier
arrival, event start and confirmed passage time
(`SewerArrival ≤ Firstquarter ≤ PassageTime`); event duration and the source
duration-dependent validity flag were not used for inclusion. **(B)** Capture
condition was unrelated to the number of post-arrival missed opening events at
the pumping station (EZ; n=35, r=**0.009**, permutation p=**0.960**) or tidal
sluice (CL; n=27, r=**−0.104**, p=**0.592**). **(C)** Condition was likewise
unrelated to post-arrival delay (EZ r=**0.072**, p=**0.677**; CL
r=**−0.182**, p=**0.350**). **(D)** At CL, the matched condition × event-duration
coefficient was near zero (β=**−0.095**, p=**0.841**, permutation p=**0.849**).
The corresponding EZ model was near-separated and non-converged and is marked
unstable rather than interpreted biologically. These independent data therefore
falsify the specific prediction that better-conditioned eels generally wait for
longer opening windows after reaching a barrier.

## Supplementary Figure S1. The original Durif-only result shows the same phase boundary

Ordinal Durif stage strongly predicted migration activation (OR **2.08**,
95% CI **1.56–2.77**) and onset (HR **1.29**, 95% CI **1.13–1.47**) but not
whole-route speed after activation (ratio **0.983**, 95% CI **0.852–1.134**) or
the frozen median positive inter-station speed endpoint (ratio **1.001**,
95% CI **0.831–1.204**).

## Supplementary Figure S2. Developmental audits do not restore a transferable positive speed gradient

Whole-route and segment-scale project heterogeneity were unsupported
(Q=**2.47**, p=**0.781**; Q=**2.32**, p=**0.804**). A simple stage-ageing
interaction was unsupported (ratio **1.036**, p=**0.382**). Inverse-probability
weighting for measured activation selection changed the stage-speed ratio only
from **1.042** to **1.062** and the condition-speed ratio from **0.907** to
**0.920**. These are selection diagnostics, not causal corrections for
unobserved readiness.

## Supplementary Figure S3. Terminal positive-set analyses remain sensitivity results only

Among activated eels, the Durif-stage odds ratio for membership in the source
terminal positive set was **1.15** (95% CI **0.83–1.59**). The historical
stacked activation-versus-terminal contrast yielded an OR ratio of **1.81**
(95% CI **1.15–2.84**). The source study did not estimate escapement success;
terminal non-membership is not interpreted as biological failure.
