# Figure captions V2 — endpoint-audited Azores manuscript

## Figure 1. Internal readiness is evaluated against migration activation and post-activation progression

**(A)** Capture-time Durif silvering stage defines the ordinal readiness axis FIII → FIV → FV.  
**(B)** Phase-resolved analysis structure: internal readiness is tested first against migration activation and threshold-defined onset, then against source-defined migration speed among activated eels. Terminal/sea endpoint observations are not treated as a complete binary failure process.  
**(C)** Six public European telemetry projects contributed 575 evaluable FIII–FV tracks after inherited source cleaning and expert corrections.

## Figure 2. Advanced Durif stage predicts whether and when behavioral migration activates

**(A)** Expert-corrected migration-activation fractions were 154/261 (59.0%) for FIII, 53/68 (77.9%) for FIV and 215/246 (87.4%) for FV. The adjusted odds ratio per one-stage increment was **2.08** (95% CI **1.56–2.76**).  
**(B)** Stratified Cox model for threshold-defined behavioral onset. The hazard ratio per stage was **1.29** (95% CI **1.13–1.47**, p = **0.00016**). Leave-one-project-out estimates are displayed as robustness diagnostics; all retained a positive direction although omission of the 2015 project reduced precision.

## Figure 3. Capture-time Durif stage does not predict a general post-activation migration-speed gradient

**(A)** Median source-defined migration speeds among activated eels retained in informative project-year strata were 0.0229 m/s for FIII, 0.0232 m/s for FIV and 0.0245 m/s for FV.  
**(B)** After project × release-year, body-length and release-timing adjustment, the multiplicative speed ratio per Durif-stage increment was **0.983** (95% CI **0.852–1.134**, p = **0.815**). The result is compatible with no transferable monotonic Durif-speed gradient; it does not prove that individual internal traits have no post-activation effects.

## Figure 4. Independent consecutive-barrier evidence identifies route-specific post-activation opportunity

Schematic of the independent Dutch source-to-sea route reported by van Rijn et al. (2026): pumping station → lake → tidal sluice → sea. Of **40** tagged FIII–FV European eels, **35** passed the pumping station and **27** were positively observed after passage through the tidal sluice/sea route; mean cumulative barrier delay was approximately **34 d**. Published analyses identified barrier-specific associations with discharge opportunity, wind, lunar illumination and prior passage experience. This system supplies independent ecological context for post-activation progression and is not a direct replication of the Europe-wide activation model.

## Supplementary Figure S1. Terminal positive-set analyses are sensitivity results only

Among activated eels, the stage odds ratio for membership in the source project's terminal positive set was **1.15** (95% CI **0.83–1.59**). The former stacked initiation-versus-terminal contrast yielded an OR ratio of **1.81** (95% CI **1.15–2.84**). These analyses are retained for reproducibility only. The source meta-analysis did not estimate escapement success rate, so terminal non-membership is not interpreted as biological failure.

## Supplementary Figure S2. Project-level route context is descriptive bridge evidence

Across six project contexts, median WRS impact had essentially no rank association with activation (rho = **0.029**, exact p = **0.983**) and a negative rank association with terminal positive-set membership among activated eels (rho = **−0.928**, exact p = **0.022**). The six contexts differ simultaneously in route, hydrology, telemetry design and terminal observability; these associations are descriptive and non-causal.
